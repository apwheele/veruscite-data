# Hallucinated Peer-Reviewed References: Recent Research & Reports

A summary of recent peer-reviewed papers, preprints, and reports documenting hallucinated/fabricated citations and their rising prevalence due to generative AI.

---

## Large-Scale Peer-Reviewed / Preprint Studies

### 1. Fabricated citations: an audit across 2.5 million biomedical papers
**Topaz, M. et al. — *The Lancet* (May 7, 2026)**
DOI: [10.1016/s0140-6736(26)00603-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0140-6736(26)00603-3)

A Columbia University School of Nursing team built an automated AI-based verification system and scanned 2.5 million papers published from January 1, 2023, to February 18, 2026, in PubMed Central's Open Access. Among 97.1 million verified references, they identified 4,046 fake citations across 2,810 papers, with the rate growing more than 12-fold since 2023 — from ~4 per 10,000 papers in 2023 to 51.3 per 10,000 in Q4 2025, reaching 56.9 per 10,000 in early 2026. 98.4% of affected papers had received no publisher action at the time of the audit.

Companion editorial: Bauchner, H. & Rivara, F.P., "Fabricated references: a new threat to editorial integrity," *The Lancet* (2026). DOI: [10.1016/s0140-6736(26)00798-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0140-6736(26)00798-1)

### 2. LLM hallucinations in the wild: Large-scale evidence from non-existent citations
**arXiv preprint (2026)**
Link: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07723

Bibliometric study estimating hallucinated citation rates across arXiv, bioRxiv, SSRN, and PMC. Found a monthly rate reaching 3,353 (arXiv), 478 (bioRxiv), 767 (SSRN), and 8,140 (PMC) in August 2025 — projecting ~146,932 hallucinated citations across these four repositories in 2025 alone, likely a lower bound on true prevalence. Found hallucinated references disproportionately credited prominent and male scholars, and were most common among early-career researchers and small teams.

Coverage: https://phys.org/news/2026-05-ai-generated-fake-citations-scientific.html

### 3. Fraudulent citations becoming more common in research papers
**STAT News (May 8, 2026)**
Link: https://www.statnews.com/2026/05/07/lancet-study-finds-steep-rise-fraudulent-citations-academic-papers/

Reports a separate dataset finding: in 2023, 1 in 2,828 papers contained one or more fabricated references; by 2025 that had reached 1 in 458 (a sixfold increase); during the first seven weeks of 2026, the rate reached 1 in 277 papers.

---

### 4. Compound Deception in Elite Peer Review: A Failure Mode Taxonomy of 100 Fabricated Citations at NeurIPS 2025
**arXiv preprint (2026)**
Link: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.05930

Builds a taxonomy of failure modes based on GPTZero's audit of NeurIPS 2025, examining how hallucinated citations evaded expert peer review (3–5 reviewers per paper).

---

### 5. Hallucinations in generative AI: A threat to scholarly integrity and the urgent need for publisher-led academically supervised verification
**ScienceDirect (2026)**
Link: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S221462962600191X

Frames hallucinated references as a cross-pipeline threat (writing, reviewing, publishing stages). Notes that GPTZero's January 2026 analysis of 4,841 NeurIPS 2025 accepted papers revealed at least 100 confirmed hallucinated citations spanning 53 papers (~1% of accepted work) despite peer review. Also reports that advanced models like GPT-4o and Claude 3.7 still exhibit 15–20% hallucination rates on factual citation tasks, rising to 35–55% on niche/recent topics.

### 6. New sources of inaccuracy? A conceptual framework for studying AI hallucinations
**HKS Misinformation Review (August 2025)**
Link: https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/new-sources-of-inaccuracy-a-conceptual-framework-for-studying-ai-hallucinations/

Conceptual framework situating citation hallucination within broader AI-driven inaccuracy, including risk of an "AI-on-AI" feedback loop where AI-generated inaccuracies pollute future training data, contributing to model collapse.

## Summary Pattern

Across these sources, a consistent picture emerges:
1. Fabricated/hallucinated citations existed at low but nonzero rates before 2023.
2. Rates have risen sharply (roughly 6x to 12x by different measures) since mid-2024, coinciding with generative AI adoption in academic writing.
3. Existing peer review and editorial safeguards are largely failing to catch these errors before publication — even at top venues with rigorous review (e.g., NeurIPS).
4. The problem spans disciplines: biomedical research, AI/ML conferences, legal filings, and government reports.
